Kybele

Radiance, custom tailored for greenhouse modeling and metrics: how much light reaches the plants, every hour of the year, from the sun and from the lamps.

Kybele models greenhouse geometry, materials, daylight and electric lighting, and reports what plants respond to: photosynthetic photon flux density (PPFD) for all 8760 hours of the year and the daily light integral (DLI) for all 365 days. Designers and growers can test a greenhouse against the light a crop needs, and see how many hours of supplemental lighting, and how much energy, it will take to close the gap.

01Plants and light

Plants see light differently

Measured in photons, not in lux

Like people, plants evolved under the natural cycle of light and dark, but they respond to it differently. Lux follows the sensitivity of the human eye, which peaks in green; plants use the whole photosynthetically active range from 400 to 700 nm, and most of all its blue and red ends.

So greenhouse light is counted in photons: PPFD (µmol/m²/s) is the flow of usable photons at a moment, and DLI (mol/m²/day) is what a plant collects over a day. Crops have their own DLI targets, which is the number Kybele checks a design against.

02Spectral conversion

From lux to photons

The conversion factor depends on the spectrum of the light

Radiance computes light in three channels, and its results are photometric (lux, weighted by the eye's V(λ)) or radiometric (W/m²). Plants need a photon count in the PAR range. Turning one into the other needs the spectral power distribution of the light, E(λ): the same lux carries more or fewer PAR photons depending on how the energy is spread across wavelengths.

For a three channel simulation the conversion is a single factor for the light source, PPFD = k × illuminance. A hyperspectral simulation carries the spectrum through the whole calculation and integrates the photons directly, so it needs no factor; that matters most for LED grow lights, whose narrow peaks a single factor cannot describe.

The factor

PPFD = ∫400700 E(λ) · λ / (h · c · NA) dλ µmol/m²/s

Ev = 683 lm/W · ∫380780 E(λ) · V(λ) dλ lux

k = PPFD / Ev µmol/m²/s per lux

h is Planck's constant, c the speed of light and NA Avogadro's number, so λ / (h c NA) is the number of moles of photons in one joule at wavelength λ (about 8.36 µmol per joule per 1000 nm). The PAR range and V(λ) are the curves in the chart above.

03The tool

What Kybele does

Rhino 8 · Grasshopper · CPython 3.9 · Radiance · EnergyPlus

Kybele works inside Rhinoceros and its visual programming environment, Grasshopper, with components written in Python. It runs the industry standard engines: Radiance for solar radiation and EnergyPlus for thermal simulation.

It joins greenhouse design practice with plant specific modelling based on PPFD and DLI requirements, so that design decisions and greenhouse operation improve together: performance, energy efficiency and plant health. Simulations run in three channels today; hyperspectral simulation is being added.

Supplemental lighting

from the DLI shortfall to hours, energy and cost

Give Kybele the crop's required DLI, the PAR output of the electric lights, the number of luminaires, their wattage and the price of electricity. For every day it reports the DLI deficit and the hours the lamps must run to make it up, then the lighting load, the annual energy use and the annual cost, ready to pass on to a thermal model.

04Case study

UW greenhouse

A two bay greenhouse among campus buildings, Seattle

The greenhouse was modelled with its glazing and structure and the surrounding buildings that shade it. Noon on the 21st of each month shows how strongly light swings through a Seattle year inside the same house, from an average of 59 µmol/m²/s in November to 1266 in August.

Over the year, the greenhouse gives on average 21.6 mol/m²/day. For a crop that needs 22 mol/m²/day all year round, it falls short on 217 days, and electric lighting at an average of 238 µmol/m²/s would have to run about 2,808 hours a year to close the gap.
